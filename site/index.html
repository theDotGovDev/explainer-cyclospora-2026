The bottom line

  • One product is doing most of the damage. Iceberg lettuce from Taylor Farms de Mexico, sourced from central Mexico, was recalled on 2026-07-17. That product is off the market. If you are not eating recalled product, your per-serving risk from any single food on this page is well under 1 in 10,000.
  • Washing is not a mitigation for this parasite. Cyclospora oocysts resist chlorine and do not rinse off cleanly. Cooking to about 158 F (70 C) and peeling are the only consumer controls that work reliably. Most "wash your produce better" advice does not apply here.
  • Substitute within produce, not away from it. Swapping iceberg for romaine or spinach, and cooking herbs instead of using them raw, captures most of the available risk reduction at essentially no nutritional cost.
  • The season is bigger than the recall. FDA has described four separate 2026 Cyclospora investigations, and most reported cases are not yet tied to any cluster. Expect further named commodities.

What the evidence does and does not show

Distinguishing confirmed findings from open questions and from historical associations is the most decision-relevant part of this page.

No product sample has tested positive for Cyclospora[26][6] Sourcing: agency + news

The link between iceberg lettuce and this outbreak rests on epidemiologic and traceback evidence, not on a laboratory detection in food. A border sample initially flagged as positive was re-reviewed by FDA laboratory experts on July 19, 2026 and reclassified as a false positive - FDA concluded the finding did not represent true amplification. FDA stated this did not change the basis for the investigation or the recall, which rest on the epidemiologic data. Cyclospora is notoriously difficult to detect in food, so the absence of a positive sample is weak evidence of absence.

There is more than one outbreak happening at once[17][20] Sourcing: news

FDA has described four separate Cyclospora outbreak investigations in 2026. Some are tied to the large Midwest iceberg lettuce cluster, some involve single states, and many reported cases are not yet linked to any cluster. The iceberg lettuce recall therefore does not account for all illness this season.

Case counts in circulation are inconsistent[1][7][23] Sourcing: agency + news

Published counts differ depending on whether they describe the whole 2026 domestically acquired season or only the iceberg lettuce cluster, and on the date of the snapshot. Outbreak-cluster counts reported in July and August 2026 ranged from roughly 1,947 illnesses in 9 states (with 98 hospitalizations) to roughly 6,358 illnesses in 15 states (with 278 hospitalizations). Treat any single number as a dated snapshot, not a current total.

Other commodities are under active traceback[17][19] Sourcing: news

As of late July 2026, traceback in some of the 2026 investigations was reported to be focused on cilantro, onions and cucumbers. Public health officials in North Carolina indicated fresh parsley and cilantro may be behind the rise in cases in that state. These are open lines of inquiry, not conclusions.

Why the usual advice does not work here

Washing
Cyclospora oocysts are highly resistant to chlorine and to routine chemical disinfection. Commercial produce washes, vinegar soaks and chlorine rinses are not reliably effective. The oocyst wall is tough and lodges in microscopic surface crevices, so it does not rinse off cleanly. CDC still recommends rinsing produce under running water, but describes disinfection as unlikely to fully eliminate Cyclospora.
Cooking
Cooking to an internal temperature of about 158 F (70 C) or higher inactivates Cyclospora. Thorough cooking is the single most reliable consumer-level control.
Peeling
Peeling physically removes the contaminated outer surface and is highly effective, provided the knife and hands do not transfer oocysts to the flesh.
Household spread
Cyclospora is not transmitted directly person-to-person in a meaningful way. Freshly passed oocysts are not infectious and require days to weeks in the environment to sporulate, so household spread from an ill person is not a significant route.
Timing of symptoms
Symptoms usually begin about one week after infection, with a range of roughly 2 days to 2 weeks or more. Untreated illness can last from a few days to a month or longer, and relapsing symptoms are common.[1][10][25][12][16] Sourcing: agency + news

When to seek care

Cyclosporiasis does not resolve reliably on its own and is treated with prescription antibiotics, so it is worth getting diagnosed rather than waiting it out. Standard stool tests do not always look for Cyclospora — it often requires a specific request or a multiplex GI panel, so mention recent produce exposure to your clinician.

Seek care promptly for watery diarrhea lasting more than a few days, diarrhea that improves and then relapses, signs of dehydration (dizziness on standing, little or no urination), fever, or any diarrheal illness if you are immunocompromised, pregnant, elderly or caring for a young child. Call 911 for severe dehydration, fainting or bloody stool.
